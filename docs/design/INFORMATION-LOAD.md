# Information density and learning load

The goal is to help a player explain their next choice and its cost without
reading the full reference material. These are design hypotheses, not measured
human-playtest outcomes. Preserve the engine, exact costs, public-information
boundaries, accessible controls, voice captions and existing saves.

## Delivery roadmap

| Step | Change | Acceptance |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1. One question per turn | Put the current stage question beside the working reading. Keep observations and available procedures together; the case record remains available. | The question follows the first unconfirmed stage using confirmed findings only. |
| 2. Teach when useful | Keep the opening about declaring and testing a reading. Explain signal/confidence on the first finding. Offer case theory and comparison when enough findings exist; early reference remains available on request. | Training starts without an expanded theory form or unusable comparison controls. Experienced players can open reference help or use existing guidance settings. |
| 3. Shorter decisions | Evidence responses show their name, a short tradeoff and exact effects. Longer descriptions and evidence/service risks sit in a separate disclosure. | Opening a rationale never selects a response. All five choices remain discoverable and keyboard reachable. Audit command/sector/response panels while preserving their existing concise descriptions and essential risk labels. |
| 4. Contextual axes | Explain signal next to its label and keep the causal-limit warning visible. Expose the current confidence basis in the report; retain the optional four-combination guide. | A player can distinguish a confirmed stage from confidence in its wider explanation. No failed roll is treated as evidence. |
| 5. Clear consequences | Lead the resolved report with the chosen response, plain-language actual meter directions and exact changes. Keep the fuller explanation available. | Summaries use recorded deltas, including unchanged meters at caps; no hidden route, score or future outcome is exposed. |

Implementation for 0.9.34 covers all five steps in the investigation and evidence-response flow. Command, sector and recovery panels retain their existing concise option descriptions and explicit effects; further shortening is a follow-up driven by beta observations.

## Follow-up measurement

An automated fresh-profile review on 16 October 2026 checked the opening on a
375 px phone and 1280 px desktop. The academy's first navigation action was
visible without scrolling on both. One wording defect was confirmed before
any check ran: the tutorial requested a source that "supports your hypothesis"
and own-source accessible names announced "supports current hypothesis".
Those descriptions now distinguish a source's ability to test a reading from
the result's support for it. A fresh-profile browser regression follows the
opening through the first procedure and its recorded turn effects, retaining
the academy and shipped guidance defaults. This is an automated review and
regression check, not evidence of newcomer comprehension or human timing.

During the public beta, record whether players can name the current question,
explain signal versus confidence, find every response and describe their
chosen tradeoff before opening its rationale. Compare observation notes with
the existing playtest protocol; record absent exposure separately. Use those
results to decide which additional command, sector or response explanations
need shortening. Do not remove strategic choices solely to reduce word count.
